# Kitchen Spanish translation validation

For sending to a Spanish-speaking former coworker. They check each translation: ✅ a real prep cook would understand and use this, ❌ wrong / would confuse, or ⚠️ technically correct but unusual / they'd say it differently.

The "Prep vocabulary" and "Measurements & quantities" sections are what we actually need verified — that's what'll be in the app. The "Service line slang" section at the bottom is just a sanity check; it won't appear in the product.

---

## SECTION 1 — Prep vocabulary (THIS IS WHAT MATTERS)

These are the words that will actually appear in prep lists and recipes inside the app.

### Cooking techniques

| English | Literal translation | Kitchen-accurate Spanish | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mince | Picar muy fino / triturar | **Picar muy fino** | "Mince" usually = very fine chop. "Triturar" implies a paste/puree, wrong here. |
| Dice | Cortar en cubos | **Cortar en cubitos** (small) / **en cubos** (large) | "Cubitos" = small dice, "cubos" = large dice. Specify size for clarity. |
| Chop | Cortar / picar | **Picar** | "Picar" is the standard verb. "Cortar" is more generic ("cut"). |
| Sauté | Saltear | **Saltear** | Same word. Borrowed from French in both languages. |
| Simmer | Hervir a fuego lento | **Cocinar a fuego lento** / **hervir a fuego lento** | Both work. "A fuego lento" is the key phrase. |
| Fold (in) | Doblar | **Incorporar con movimientos envolventes** | LITERAL "doblar" is wrong — that means "fold a napkin." Real cooking term is much longer. Worth shortening to **"incorporar suavemente"** in app. |
| Whisk (verb) | Batir | **Batir** | Same word. Standard. |
| Whisk (noun, the tool) | Batidor | **Batidor** / **globo** | "Batidor" is standard. "Globo" is the balloon whisk specifically. |
| Deglaze | Desglasar | **Desglasar** | Direct cognate. Standard professional term. |
| Blanch | Blanquear | **Blanquear** / **escaldar** | Both used. "Escaldar" is slightly more common in home/restaurant Spanish. |
| Sear | Sellar / dorar | **Sellar** | "Sellar" = sear (seal the meat). "Dorar" = brown more generally. |
| Reduce | Reducir | **Reducir** | Direct cognate. Standard. |
| Strain | Colar | **Colar** | Standard. |
| Season | Sazonar / condimentar | **Sazonar** | Both work. "Sazonar" is more common in pro kitchens. |
| Marinate | Marinar | **Marinar** | Direct cognate. |
| Portion | Porcionar | **Porcionar** | Direct cognate. Standard. |
| Brunoise | Brunoise | **Brunoise** | French term, used as-is in both languages. |
| Chiffonade | Chifonada | **Chifonada** / **chiffonade** | Often kept in French. |
| Julienne | Juliana | **Cortar en juliana** | Standard. |

### Equipment / kitchen locations

| English | Literal translation | Kitchen-accurate Spanish | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk-in (cooler) | Caminar adentro | **El walk-in** / **el cuarto frío** / **la cámara** | **"Caminar adentro" is completely wrong** — that's the literal action, not the room. In US kitchens, most Spanish-speaking staff just say "el walk-in." "Cuarto frío" is the formal Spanish term. **Recommend using "cuarto frío" in app** since it's correct Spanish, not Spanglish. |
| Reach-in (cooler) | n/a | **El refrigerador** / **la nevera** | Just "the fridge." |
| Freezer | Congelador | **El congelador** | Standard. |
| Cutting board | Tabla de cortar | **Tabla de cortar** / **tabla** | Standard. |
| Sheet pan | Charola / bandeja | **Charola** (Mexican Spanish) / **bandeja** (more European) | Region-dependent. **"Charola"** is most common in US kitchens given workforce demographics. |
| Pot | Olla | **Olla** | Standard. |
| Pan / skillet | Sartén | **Sartén** | Standard. |
| Container (deli/Cambro) | Recipiente | **Recipiente** / **el cambro** | "Cambro" is a brand name used generically, like Kleenex. Both will be understood. |
| Quart container | Cuarto de galón | **Recipiente de un cuarto** / **cuarto** | "Cuarto" alone often works in context. |

## SECTION 3 — Sample prep list phrases (full sentences, the real test)

This is the closest test to what the app will actually produce. These are full prep-list-style sentences, ready for end-to-end verification.

1. **EN:** Dice 4 cases of yellow onions, small dice. Store in quart containers.
   **ES:** Cortar 4 cajas de cebolla amarilla en cubitos pequeños. Guardar en recipientes de un cuarto.

2. **EN:** Make 6 quarts of ranch dressing. See recipe.
   **ES:** Preparar 6 cuartos de aderezo ranch. Ver la receta.

3. **EN:** Portion chicken breast into 6 oz pieces. Label and date. Store in walk-in.
   **ES:** Porcionar la pechuga de pollo en piezas de 6 onzas. Etiquetar y poner la fecha. Guardar en el cuarto frío.

4. **EN:** Mince 2 cups of garlic. Cover with olive oil.
   **ES:** Picar 2 tazas de ajo muy fino. Cubrir con aceite de oliva.

5. **EN:** Blanch 3 cases of green beans. Shock in ice water. Drain and store.
   **ES:** Blanquear 3 cajas de ejotes. Enfriar en agua con hielo. Escurrir y guardar.

6. **EN:** Reduce 2 gallons of stock to half. Strain and cool.
   **ES:** Reducir 2 galones de caldo a la mitad. Colar y enfriar.

7. **EN:** Whisk together heavy cream and sugar to soft peaks. Refrigerate.
   **ES:** Batir la crema espesa con el azúcar hasta picos suaves. Refrigerar.

8. **EN:** Marinate 10 lbs of skirt steak overnight. Use marinade #3.
   **ES:** Marinar 10 libras de arrachera durante la noche. Usar el marinado #3.

9. **EN:** Sauté onions and peppers until soft. Season to taste.
   **ES:** Saltear las cebollas y los pimientos hasta que estén suaves. Sazonar al gusto.

10. **EN:** Fold cilantro into the cooled rice. Cover and refrigerate.
    **ES:** Incorporar el cilantro al arroz frío con movimientos suaves. Tapar y refrigerar.

## SECTION 4 — Service line slang (NOT in the app, just curiosity check)

These won't appear in prep lists. Included only to confirm understanding of how line/service Spanish differs from prep Spanish. Skip if pressed for time.

| English | What it literally means | What Spanish-speaking line cooks actually say |
|---|---|---|
| Fire (the apps) | n/a — service command meaning "start cooking" | **"Vámonos con..."** or just **"fuego"** in some kitchens. No clean translation. |
| 86 (the salmon) | n/a — out of stock | **"Ya no hay salmón"** / **"se acabó"**. No equivalent of "86" exists in Spanish. |
| Heard / yes chef | acknowledgment | **"Sí chef"** / **"oído"** ("heard"). "Sí chef" is universal. |
| Behind | I'm passing behind you | **"Detrás"** / **"atrás"**. Often kept in English in mixed-language kitchens. |
| Corner | I'm coming around the corner | **"Esquina"**. Same. |
| Expo | the expediter / pass | **"Expo"** — kept in English. |
| On the fly | rush order, NOW | **"Al vuelo"** (literal). In practice, often kept in English. |
| Ten-top | table of 10 | **"Mesa de diez"**. |
| Dish | dishwasher position | **"Lavaplatos"** (the person) / **"el dish"** in slang. |
| SOS | sauce on the side | No equivalent. Spanish-speaking staff usually pick this up as English jargon. |

The pattern: **service slang doesn't translate, it gets learned as English jargon by Spanish-speaking staff.** Prep language, by contrast, has real and standard Spanish equivalents.

This is the key insight for the product: the translation problem you actually have to solve is the prep vocabulary, and it's *much* more tractable than the slang would suggest.
