# Discovery call playbook — realtor interviews
 
**Goal of these calls:** Validate (or invalidate) the pain points and module priority in our product spec. Identify which problems are real, which are imagined, and which we underweighted. Find our first 2-3 design partners among willing agents.
 
**Hard rule:** Do not pitch. Do not validate. Do not lead. The best interview is one where the agent talks 90% of the time and we leave with a clearer picture than we arrived with.
 
---
 
## Pre-call (5 min before)
 
- Pull up agent's LinkedIn, website, recent listings — note 1-2 things to reference naturally
- Check what brokerage they're at, roughly how long they've been in business
- Have this doc open in another window
- Partner: confirm AI note-taker is armed and ready to join the call for transcript + summary
- Decide who leads questions (Elijah by default) and who watches for follow-ups / emotional cues (partner)
---
 
## The call structure (15-20 min total)
 
### Opening (1 min)
 
> "Thanks so much for making time, [name]. Quick context: my partner [partner's name] and I are early-stage on a project for residential agents. We're not selling anything today and we're not going to demo anything — we just want to understand how your day actually works and where the friction is. Whatever you tell us shapes what we build, so the more honest the better. Heads up — [partner's name] uses an AI tool that joins the call to take notes for us, just so we don't miss anything. We won't share any of it. That cool with you?"
 
If they say no to the AI tool: have your partner kick it from the call, switch to manual notes. One of you fully on listening, the other writing as fast as possible. The conversation matters more than the recording.
 
### Warm-up (1-2 min)
 
One soft question to get them talking. Pick whichever fits:
 
- "How long have you been at [brokerage]?"
- "How's the market feeling for you right now?"
The goal is just to get them into talking mode. Don't overstay here.
 
### Core questions (12-15 min)
 
Ask these mostly in order. **Let them go on tangents — that's where the gold is.** Use follow-up prompts liberally. If they answer two questions at once, skip the second one.
 
**1. Walk me through a typical week — where does most of your time actually go?**
- *Follow-ups:* "What part of that feels like the worst use of your time?" / "What did you spend the most time on this week specifically?"
- *Listen for:* Specific tasks they hate, hours per week on admin vs. selling, what they wish they could outsource
**2. When you get a new listing, what's the process from signed agreement to everything live and marketed? How long does that take you?**
- *Follow-ups:* "What's the most painful part of that?" / "What do you skip because you don't have time?"
- *Listen for:* Whether the Listing Launch Engine pain is real, what assets they actually create vs. wish they created, how long it really takes
**3. How consistent are you with posting content right now? What gets in the way?**
- *Follow-ups:* "When you do post, what does that process look like?" / "What's stopped you from being more consistent?"
- *Listen for:* Whether they actually care or just say they care, what they've tried before, where their voice/brand insecurity lives
**4. Do you send market updates to your database? If not — what stops you?**
- *Follow-ups:* "What do your clients ask for that you wish you could deliver easily?" / "When was the last one you sent?"
- *Listen for:* Whether this is genuine demand or just a "should." The "I'll send something soon" line from the ICP doc.
**5. When was the last time you built a CMA from scratch? How long did it take?**
- *Follow-ups:* "What does a good one look like to you?" / "Do you ever lose listings to agents with better presentations?"
- *Listen for:* Time investment, whether they think they win/lose listings on this, what "good" means
**6. What tools are you currently using for marketing and content — and which ones are you actually using versus just paying for?**
- *Follow-ups:* "Which one did you most regret signing up for?" / "What was the last tool you canceled and why?"
- *Listen for:* Tool fatigue, churn patterns, price ceilings, what made them stick or not
**7. If you had an extra 10 hours a month back, what would you spend them on?**
- *Listen for:* Where they think the real ROI is — prospecting? client time? family? This tells you what time savings actually mean to them in dollars.
**8. Have you tried using AI tools like ChatGPT for your business? What happened?**
- *Follow-ups:* "What did you try to use it for?" / "Why did you stop?" (assume they stopped)
- *Listen for:* AI skepticism, "sounds like a robot" objection, what good AI output looks like to them
**9. What would a tool have to do for you to consider it a non-negotiable part of your business?**
- *Listen for:* The bar. What "indispensable" looks like. Compare to what they say about their current CRM.
**10. If we built something that handled [reference their top 2-3 pain points from earlier in the call] — and it took 20 minutes to set up — what would make you say no?**
- *This is the only "test" question. It's a soft commitment ask, not a pitch.*
- *Listen for:* Real objections. Price, trust, time, skepticism. Don't argue. Just absorb.
- *If they say "nothing, sign me up":* "Would you actually be open to being one of the first to try it once we have something? No commitment, just want to know if you're game."
### Closing (1-2 min)
 
> "Last question — is there anyone else you'd recommend we talk to? Another agent who'd have strong opinions about this stuff?"
 
This is critical. **Always ask for referrals.** Your list of 5 becomes 8 becomes 15.
 
Then:
 
> "Really appreciate the time. We'll send you a quick note in a few weeks once we have something to show you. If anything else comes to mind that we should know, just shoot me a text."

## Follow-up prompts to use anywhere
 
When you want them to go deeper, in rough order of usefulness:
 
- "Tell me more about that."
- "When was the last time that happened?"
- "What did you do instead?"
- "What would have to be true for that to work?"
- "Has anyone ever solved that for you?"
- "Why do you think that is?"
- "What did you try?"
- "How much time / money did that cost you?"
- "What's the cost of not solving it?"
When they say something interesting and you want to make sure you got it right:
 
- "So if I'm hearing you right — [paraphrase]. Is that fair?"
When they go off on a tangent that's actually useful — let them. When they go off on a tangent that's not useful — gently redirect:
 
- "That's helpful context. Going back to [topic] for a second..."

## What to capture, not just transcribe
 
In your notes during/after the call, organize what you heard into these buckets. The transcript is just raw material — the synthesis is what matters.
 
### Direct quotes worth remembering
Verbatim language they used about their pain. This is your marketing copy later.
 
### Pain ranked by intensity
Which pains made them visibly frustrated? Which were "yeah I guess that's annoying"? Heat matters more than what they said.
 
### Current workarounds
What are they doing today instead? VAs, tools, just suffering, ignoring the problem? The workaround tells you the real value of solving it.
 
### Surprises
What did they say that contradicted our assumptions? **This is the most important section.** If nothing surprised you, you weren't listening hard enough.
 
### Module reaction signal
For each of the 7 modules, did they organically mention the underlying pain? Rate 1-3:
- 3 = brought it up unprompted, visibly frustrated
- 2 = nodded along when I mentioned it but didn't surface it themselves
- 1 = barely registered or seemed to disagree it's a problem
### Price signal
Did they react to any tool prices they mentioned? Did they say a number? Did they wince at $200/month or shrug?
 
### Would they actually pay
Gut score 1-5: how likely are they to actually open their wallet for this? Not "did they say yes" — your gut after talking to them.
 
### Are they a potential design partner
Y/N — would you want to keep talking to this specific person as you build?

## Post-call (within 24 hours)
 
1. **AI summary is raw material, not synthesis.** Partner's AI will spit out a transcript and summary automatically. That's the starting point, not the deliverable. Generic AI summaries ("the agent discussed marketing challenges") miss what actually matters — emotional weight, contradictions, surprises.
2. **Both of you fill out the capture template independently first.** Each of you spends 10 min writing your own version of: top quotes, pain ranked by intensity, surprises, module reaction signal, gut score on willingness to pay. Don't read the AI summary or each other's notes yet — fresh impressions matter.
3. **Then compare:** AI summary + Elijah's notes + partner's notes side by side. The disagreements are where the real insights live. If you both noticed the same pain but ranked it differently, dig into why. If the AI missed something you both caught, that's a pattern.
4. **Send a thank-you text:**
   > "Hey [name] — really appreciated the time today, you gave us a lot to think about. Will keep you posted as we figure out what we're actually building. Owe you a coffee sometime."
5. **Add them to a tracking sheet** with: date called, status (interested/not/maybe), would they pay, would they design-partner, transcript link, synthesis notes link.
6. **After every 2-3 calls, do a synthesis pass together.** What's repeating? What's contradicting our assumptions? What changed in your gut? This is where strategic decisions actually get made — not in any single call.

## Red flags to watch for in yourself (and your partner)
 
- **Pitching when they ask "so what are you building?"** — Answer briefly and redirect: "Honestly, we're trying to figure that out — that's why this conversation matters. Going back to [their last pain point]..."
- **Leading questions.** "Don't you hate when..." vs. "Tell me about..."
- **Validating instead of probing.** When they describe a problem, your instinct will be to say "yes! that's exactly what we're solving!" Don't. Ask another question instead.
- **Defending the product when they push back.** If they say "I'd never pay for that," your job is to ask why, not to convince them otherwise.
- **Talking over each other.** Decide before each call who leads the questions and who takes notes / asks follow-ups. Switch up across calls if you want, but don't both try to drive.
---
 
## After all 5 calls
 
Aggregate into a single doc with:
- The 3 pains that came up most often, ranked by intensity
- The pains we assumed mattered that didn't
- The pains we missed entirely
- Pricing signal (what did people react to?)
- 2-3 quotes that capture the ICP's voice
- Module priority recommendation — does v1 spec hold up?
- List of agents willing to be design partners
This is the doc that determines what we actually build.
